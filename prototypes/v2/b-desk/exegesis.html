A Preliminary Mathematical Exegesis of Diffusion Models

Gaining Clarity From True Understanding

Shuyue Wang 王舒悦 · ORCID · 2026 · working draft

A short, mathematically serious book about how diffusion models actually work: why natural images occupy a vanishing fraction of pixel space, how the ELBO becomes a tractable objective, and how denoising turns into score estimation.

About the book

Generative AI has transformed countless aspects of our world. Among its many techniques, the diffusion model stands out as a groundbreaking framework—powering innovations like Stable Diffusion, whose magic captivates anyone (myself included) who remembers a time before the AI revolution.

For learners like me, most tutorials and code snippets in blogs and papers provide only fragmented insights—forcing us to piece together scattered, incomplete explanations. I’ve tried to write it like something where ideas build naturally.

Contents

  1. 00

    Preface

    Why the book exists, and how to read it. The gap between “I can run the code” and “I can derive the objective”; the few deliberate deus ex machina assumptions that make the derivation possible.

    Free sample

  2. I

    The High-Dimensional Structure of True Data

    Natural images are a vanishingly small subset of all pixel arrays, so a Gaussian fit collapses into blur. The chapter builds the geometric picture: pdata, the concentration of high-dimensional noise, the thin shell, and the measure-theoretic foundations needed later.

    Free sample

  3. II

    The ELBO Paradigm

    Latent variables enter as the first modeling device. Through the cave analogy, the chapter separates the observed image from its hidden causes, derives the ELBO, and reads the KL gap as the price of using a proxy objective instead of the intractable likelihood.

  4. III

    Dissecting the ELBO

    The longest chapter turns the ELBO into diffusion. Multiple latents, a Gaussian noising encoder, the central limit theorem, Tweedie’s formula, the score function, Fisher divergence, the Fokker–Planck equation, and score matching are assembled into one chain: denoising is score estimation.

  5. IV

    Implementation on Machine

    From the reverse SDE to a discrete update: the forward schedule, ᾱ, the ε-prediction loss, and the DDPM-style sampling step. This chapter is being expanded with a walk through a real latent-diffusion codebase.

What you’ll learn

  • Why natural images are rare in pixel space, and why that rules out a naive Gaussian model.
  • How to derive the ELBO from scratch and explain the KL gap without hand-waving.
  • Why the forward process ends in a Gaussian, and what the central limit theorem has to do with it.
  • How Tweedie’s formula connects denoising to the score function.
  • How the Fokker–Planck equation turns a distribution transition into a tractable training objective.
  • How to discretize the reverse SDE into a DDPM-style sampler.

FAQ

Who is this book for?

Graduate students, engineers, and researchers who want the mathematics behind diffusion models to click. It assumes basic probability, linear algebra, and calculus; it does not assume a background in diffusion models.

How is it different from other diffusion tutorials?

It follows one continuous derivation instead of collecting tricks. Each deus ex machina assumption is stated and justified, and the goal is to show why each step is needed, not just what the update rule is.
