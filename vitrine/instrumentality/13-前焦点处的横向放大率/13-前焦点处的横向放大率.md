# Transverse magnification at the front focal point: why $m=h'/h$ diverges to $+\infty$

> Question: For the transverse magnification $m=h'/h$, when the object is placed at the front focal point $F$ (the object-side focal point), why does $m$ tend to infinity? In this case, what are $h'$ and $h$?

## 0. Conclusion

When the object lies exactly at the front focal point $F$ (i.e., the object plane coincides with the front focal plane):

- The emergent rays are parallel; the image is at infinity, i.e., the image distance $s'\to\infty$;
- The transverse magnification diverges, $m=h'/h\to\infty$ (more precisely $|m|\to\infty$, with the sign depending on the direction of approach);
- $h$ remains finite and nonzero, while $h'=m\,h$ therefore diverges—**there is no real image at a finite location with finite size**.

This "infinity" is meaningful as a limit: it is the idealized limit as the object distance tends to the focal length, not an observable object–image relation.

## 1. Sign convention

We adopt a signed Cartesian sign convention, with the positive direction along the principal optical axis pointing to the right:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $O$ | Optical center of the thin lens, taken as the coordinate origin $x=0$ |
| $s$ | Object distance (signed): real object $s<0$, virtual object $s>0$ |
| $s'$ | Image distance (signed): real image $s'>0$, virtual image $s'<0$ |
| $f$ | Focal length (signed): convex lens $f>0$; with air on both sides $f'=f$ |
| $F$ | Front focal point (object-side focal point), coordinate $x_F=-f$ |
| $F'$ | Back focal point (image-side focal point), coordinate $x_{F'}=f$ |
| $h$ | Object height (signed); $h'>0$ means erect |
| $m=h'/h$ | Transverse magnification |

> **Reference origin**: $s$ and $s'$ are both measured from the optical center $O$—for a thin lens, the two principal planes coincide with this plane (i.e., with $O$); for a thick lens or a lens system, $s$ and $s'$ are generally measured from the object-side principal plane $H$ and the image-side principal plane $H'$, respectively. The Newtonian coordinate $x$, by contrast, is measured from the front focal point $F$; see §2.2.

> Note: The derivation uses only the geometry of two characteristic rays (§2); the Gaussian imaging equation is not needed.

## 2. Two forms of the transverse magnification

### 2.1 Gaussian form

Using the ray through the optical center $O$ (which is undeviated), similar triangles give
$$
m=\frac{h'}{h}=\frac{s'}{s}. \tag{1}
$$

For example, with the object at twice the focal length, $s=-2f$ (object and image symmetric about the lens), $s'=2f$, and $m=-1$: an inverted, same-size real image, where the minus sign indicates inversion.

### 2.2 Newtonian form

Measure the signed distance from the front focal point:
$$
x=s-x_F=s+f. \tag{2}
$$
For an object point at height $h$ in the object plane, take the **ray through the front focal point $F$**: owing to the focal-point property, this ray emerges parallel to the principal optical axis after the lens. The similar triangle with vertex at $F$ gives the signed height of this ray when it reaches the lens as $h\,f/x$; after emergence, the height of this parallel ray equals the image height $h'$, hence
$$
m=\frac{h'}{h}=\frac{f}{x}. \tag{3}
$$

> Note: Some textbooks define $x$ as a positive distance measured to the left from $F$ (opposite to the direction of light propagation), in which case Eq. (3) is written as $m=-f/x$. The sign difference stems from the positive-direction convention for $x$; the divergence conclusion is unaffected—see Section 3.

## 3. Object exactly at the front focal point: why $m\to\infty$

### 3.1 Physical interpretation: image at infinity

Light emitted from an object point located exactly at $F$ emerges parallel to the principal optical axis after the lens; light from an object point located anywhere in the front focal plane emerges, after the lens, as a family of parallel rays (parallel to the inclination of the line joining that point to the optical center). Parallel rays "intersect" only at infinity, corresponding to:

- image distance $s'=\infty$;
- no real image at any finite distance.

When the object is treated as a segment of finite height $h\neq0$ placed in the front focal plane, the emergent light from each object point remains parallel and the geometric relations among the points are unchanged—the image "unfolds" at infinity, formally expressed as $h'\to\infty$. From Eq. (1), in $m=s'/s$ we have $s'=\infty$ and finite $s=-f$, so $|m|=|s'/s|\to\infty$.

### 3.2 Algebraic derivation

An object at the front focal point $F$ means $s=x_F=-f$; the Newtonian coordinate is
$$
x=s+f=0.
$$
Substituting into Eq. (3):
$$
m=\frac{f}{x}=\frac{f}{0}\to\infty. \tag{4}
$$
Approaching from $x\to0^-$ (object just outside the focal plane; the real-image branch) and from $x\to0^+$ (object just inside the focal plane; the virtual-image branch), $m$ tends to $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, respectively; that is, $|m|\to\infty$, with the sign depending on the direction of approach.

## 4. What are $h'$ and $h$ in this case

| Quantity | Value | Description |
|---|---|---|
| $h$ | finite, nonzero | The actual height of the object, a directly measurable geometric quantity |
| $h'$ | $\infty$ (in the limit sense) | $h'=m\,h$; it diverges because $m\to\infty$ |

**Precise statement**: $h'\to\infty$ does not mean that an "enormous image" exists at a finite location; rather, it refers to the following limiting process—as the object distance $s$ tends to $-f$ from either side (i.e., as the object plane approaches the front focal plane), the image distance and the image height diverge simultaneously, and the rate of divergence satisfies $m=h'/h\sim f/x$. At the isolated point where the object distance equals the focal length exactly, the optical system yields no finite real image at all, so $h'$ takes no finite value there.

If the object degenerates completely into an axial point ($h=0$), its image likewise degenerates into a single ray along the axis, and the ratio $m$ becomes a $0/0$ indeterminate form; in that case it is preferable to describe the situation with the angular magnification (emergent angle / incident angle), and the transverse magnification is no longer discussed.

## 6. Common confusions

1. **"The magnification is infinite" does not mean "an infinitely large real image appears."** It characterizes the divergence of the image distance: the image recedes to infinity and is magnified without bound as the object plane approaches the front focal plane.
2. **No finite real image is formed at the front focal point.** With the object at the focal point, the image distance $\to\infty$, corresponding to the vertical asymptote $l=-f$ in the curve of the object–image position relation.
3. **The sign convention is irrelevant.** Whether one adopts $m=-f/x$ or $m=f/x$, the conclusion $|m|\to\infty$ as $x\to0$ is unchanged.
